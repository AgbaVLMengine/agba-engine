### Pillar I: Ìtìrẹ́-Ilé (Ado-Odo Area, Ogun State) — The Primordial Womb
In Yorùbá settlement morphology, the suffix **"-Ilé"** (as in *Ọ̀yọ́-Ilé*, *Òwu-Ilé*, *Ìtìrẹ́-Ilé*) designates the Original Ancestral Mother-Settlement from which later branches migrated. 
When the royal prince-hunters descended from the interior under the sovereign aegis of Ọlọ́fin Ògúnfúnminíre, they first established their ancient seats and shrines in the fertile Ado-Odo forested corridor. That sanctuary is **Ìtìrẹ́-Ilé**—the ancestral home housing the primordial oaths and foundational stool.

### Pillar II: Ìbàsà (The Maritime Creek Network) — The Naval Outpost
Located along the historic Badagry/Ojo coastal lagoon waterways, Ìbàsà served as the strategic maritime station. Princes of the Onítìrẹ́ lineage were stationed here to govern coastal water trade, manage fish traps (*Àkùrọ̀*), and safeguard the maritime approaches to the island of Èkó. To this day, the Onítìrẹ́ royal family retains unbroken landholding rights, shrines, and sacred kinship ties in Ìbàsà.

### Pillar III: Ìtìrẹ́ (Lagos Mainland) — The Sovereign Metropolitan Throne
As Lagos expanded into the mainland hinterland, the Onítìrẹ́ royal line crossed the lagoon waters to establish the paramount mainland seat at **Ìtìrẹ́** (the modern realm bordering Surulere, Mushin, and Lawanson). Here, the reigning monarch was crowned **Ọba Onítìrẹ́ of Ìtìrẹ́**, preserving the royal panegyric:

### 2.4 The Exodus of the Fallen Tree & The Founding of Agbóle Ọmọ́taṣe

An essential, sacred oral tradition of the Onítìrẹ́ dynasty—preserved directly in the living memory of the Lead Architect’s royal family—records the exact catalyst for the physical migration from Ìtìrẹ́-Ilé to the mainland:

1. **The Royal Taboo of the Sacred Crown (*Èèwọ̀ Ọba*):**

2. **The Palace Tree Tragedy at Ìtìrẹ́-Ilé:**  
   In ancient Ìtìrẹ́-Ilé, a massive sacred tree standing directly before the palace gates began to lean precariously. As loyal palace helpers and royal attendants mobilized to brace and cut it, the tree tragically collapsed upon them, killing them instantly before the palace entrance.

3. **The Immediate Royal Exodus:**  
   Forbidden by cosmic law from gazing upon the fallen corpses, the reigning monarch immediately gathered his sacred beaded crown (*Adé*), the royal scepter of office (*Ọ̀pá Àṣẹ*), and his immediate royal family, departing without hesitation out the back gates of the palace.

4. **The Second King of Ìtìrẹ́ & Agbóle Ọmọ́taṣe:**  
   Following the creek routes through Ìbàsà, the royal family crossed onto the fertile Lagos mainland, founding the metropolitan citadel of **Ìtìrẹ́**. The founding king was succeeded by his illustrious son, **Ọba Ọmọ́taṣe**—the second Ọba of Ìtìrẹ́ and the Great-Great-Great-Grandfather of Lead Architect **Àrùnà Ọlànrewájú Kábírù**. To this day, the ancestral royal family compound in Ìtìrẹ́ stands as **Agbóle Ọmọ́taṣe** (The Royal Compound of Ọmọ́taṣe), the living heart of Kábíyèsí's royal lineage.
