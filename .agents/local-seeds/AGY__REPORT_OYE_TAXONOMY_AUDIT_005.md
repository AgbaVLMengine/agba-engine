1. **Sacred Association Fallacy**: If an entity possessed any sacred, ritual, or mythological attribution (e.g. `Ẹja` offered to Ọ̀ṣun/Yemọja, `Ìyẹ̀rosùn` powder spread on Ọpọ́n Ifá, `Orin Ọ̀ṣun` sung during river rites, `Ọ̀bẹ` forged under Ògún, `Ọ̀ṣọ́gbó Grove` sacred to Ọ̀ṣun), earlier ingestion pipelines dumped it into `Spiritual & Cosmological Matrix`, swelling Category 1 to **434 entities** (nearly 33% of the corpus).
2. **Token & Homograph Traps**:
   - `Dùndú` (deep-fried yam slices) was filed under `Military Traditions, Weaponry & Defense` due to token confusion with `Dùndún` (the talking war drum).
   - `Agbada-Aro` (a shallow cast-iron frying pan / clay roasting griddle) was filed under `Attire, Textiles & Personal Adornment` because `Agbada` was matched to the flowing three-piece robe.
   - `Obẹ-Ata` (foundational red pepper stew) was filed under `Social Structure, Kinship & Lineage`.
   - `Ìnwálẹ̀` (agricultural tuber harvesting spade) was filed under `Culinary Arts & Indigenous Gastronomy`.
3. **Ontological Conflation**: An animal is an animal (*Erán/Ẹranko/Ẹyẹ/Ẹja*); a cooking utensil is a culinary implement; an iron knife is a blacksmith's tool; and a playground singing rhyme is an auditory musical art. A sacred association belongs as an edge in the knowledge graph, never as the primary ontological category.
