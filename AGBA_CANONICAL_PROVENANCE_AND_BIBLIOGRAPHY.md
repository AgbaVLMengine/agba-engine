## 📚 2. Academic Master Bibliography (Foundational Literature)

### A. Classical History, Monarchy & Dynastic Chronicles
1. **Johnson, Rev. Samuel (1921).** *The History of the Yorubas from the Earliest Times to the Beginning of the British Protectorate.* London: George Routledge & Sons.
   - *Engine Coverage:* The structural foundation for all Ọ̀yọ́ dynastic chronologies, imperial offices (`Ààrẹ Ọ̀nà Kakañfò`, `Ọ̀yọ́ Mèsì`), martial history, and early kingdom formations.
2. **Smith, Robert S. (1988).** *Kingdoms of the Yoruba.* 3rd ed. Madison: University of Wisconsin Press.
   - *Engine Coverage:* Interstate diplomacy, city-state fortifications (`Odi`), warfare mechanisms, and treaty protocols between Èkìtì Parapọ̀, Ẹ̀gbá, Ìjẹ̀bú, and Ìbàdàn.
3. **Akíntóyè, S. A. (1971).** *Revolution and Power Politics in Yorubaland 1840–1893.* London: Longman.
   - *Engine Coverage:* 19th-century military confederacies, the Kiriji War, and the rise of autonomous war-chief republics.

### B. Divination Matrices, Cosmology & Indigenous Philosophy
4. **Abímbọ́lá, Wándé (1975).** *Sixteen Great Poems of Ifá.* Paris: UNESCO / Niamey: CELHTO.
   - *Engine Coverage:* Tonal orthography, verbatim poetic structure of the 16 Principal Odù (`Èjì Ogbe` to `Òfún Méjì`), and divinatory ethics.
5. **Abímbọ́lá, Wándé (1976).** *Ifá: An Exposition of Ifá Literary Corpus.* Ibadan: Oxford University Press Nigeria.
   - *Engine Coverage:* Epistemological foundations of *Àyànmọ́* (destiny), *Orí-Inú* (the metaphysical inner head), and sacrificial remedies.
6. **Bascom, William (1969).** *Ifa Divination: Communication Between Gods and Men in West Africa.* Bloomington: Indiana University Press.
   - *Engine Coverage:* Mathematical casting mechanics of `Ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀` chains and `Ikin` sacred palm nuts on the `Ọpọ́n Ifá`.
7. **Bascom, William (1980).** *Sixteen Cowries: Yoruba Divination from Africa to the New World.* Bloomington: Indiana University Press.
   - *Engine Coverage:* The `Ẹẹ́rìndínlógún` cowrie-shell divination system, diasporic preservation, and transatlantic liturgical continuity.
8. **Ìdòwú, E. Bọ́lájì (1962).** *Olódùmarè: God in Yoruba Belief.* London: Longmans.
   - *Engine Coverage:* Primordial theology, the celestial hierarchy of the Òrìṣà (`Ọ̀rúnmìlà`, `Ògún`, `Ọbàtálá`, `Ṣàngó`, `Ọ̀ṣun`), and metaphysical covenants.
9. **Oluwole, Sophie B. (2014).** *Socrates and Ọ̀rúnmìlà: Two Patron Saints of Classical Philosophy.* Lagos: Ark Publishers.
   - *Engine Coverage:* Formal validation of indigenous Yorùbá philosophy as a rationalist, democratic, and epistemological school of thought.

### C. Visual Semiotics, Beadwork, Sculpture & Aesthetics
10. **Abiodun, Rowland (2014).** *Yoruba Art and Language: Seeking the African in African Art.* Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.
    - *Engine Coverage:* The master theoretical taxonomy for Àgbà Engine aesthetics: *Ojú-Inú* (inner vision), *Àṣẹ* (performative command), *Ìwà* (character as beauty), and *Àkó* (second-burial effigies).
11. **Drewal, Henry John, and John Pemberton III (1989).** *Yoruba: Nine Centuries of African Art and Thought.* New York: Center for African Art & Harry N. Abrams.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Classical 12th–15th century Ilé-Ifẹ̀ copper-alloy lost-wax casting, terracotta crowns, and 19th-century royal regalia.
12. **Lawal, Babatunde (1996).** *The Gẹ̀lẹ̀dẹ́ Spectacle: Art, Gender, and Social Harmony in an African Culture.* Seattle: University of Washington Press.
    - *Engine Coverage:* The sacred society of *Àwọn Ìyá Wa* (Our Mothers), mask iconography, maternal balance, and nocturnal *Ẹfẹ̀* ceremonies.
13. **Lawal, Babatunde (2001).** *Awòrán: Representing the Self and Its Metaphysical Other in Yoruba Art.* The Art Bulletin 83, no. 3: 498–526.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Anthropomorphic abstraction, `Orí-Òde` vs. `Orí-Inú`, and facial scarification markers (`Gọ̀mbọ́`, `Pélé`, `Àbàjà`).

### D. Social Structure, Gender, Textiles & Ethnobotany
14. **Oyěwùmí, Oyèrónkẹ́ (1997).** *The Invention of Women: Making an African Sense of Western Gender Discourses.* Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Structural clarity that Yorùbá social organization is organized along chronological seniority (*Ẹgbẹ́*) rather than anatomical gender.
15. **Eicher, Joanne Bubolz (1976).** *Nigerian Handcrafted Textiles.* Ile-Ife: University of Ife Press.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Weaving structures of hand-spun wild silk (`Sányán`), red camwood silk (`Àlàárì`), and indigo bark-cloth (`Ẹtù`).
16. **Verger, Pierre Fatumbi (1995).** *Ewé: The Use of Plants in Yoruba Society.* São Paulo: Odebrecht & Paris: Editions M.H.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Indigenous botanical pharmacopeia, sacred leaves (`Ewé Òdúndún`, `Ewé Tẹ̀tẹ̀`, `Ewé Rinrin`), and incantatory herbal preparations (`Àgbo`).

### E. 19th-Century Warfare, Urban Sociology, Guilds & Kinship Law (Batches 1–3 Additions)
17. **Fadipe, N. A. (1970).** *The Sociology of the Yoruba.* Ibadan: Ibadan University Press.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Foundational sociological structure of the compound (`Agbo-Ilé`), lineage headship (`Mègàjí` / `Baálé`), kinship solidarity (`Aṣọ-Ẹbí`), and dispute arbitration.
18. **Lloyd, P. C. (1962).** *Yoruba Land Law.* London: Oxford University Press.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Indigenous property jurisprudence, ancestral land tenure, lineage inheritance covenants (`Oogun`), and council hall governance (`Ilé-Akodi`).
19. **Falola, Toyin (1984).** *The Political Economy of a Pre-colonial African State: Ibadan, 1830–1900.* Ile-Ife: University of Ife Press.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Military republicanism, warrior lineages (`Balógun Ìbíkúnlé`, `Balógun Ògúnmọ́lá`), trade routes, and weapons technology.
20. **Falola, Toyin (1999).** *Pioneer, Patriot and Patriarch: Samuel Johnson and the Yoruba.* Madison: African Studies Program, University of Wisconsin-Madison.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Historiographical methodology and provenance validation of the 1897/1921 Samuel Johnson foundational corpus.
21. **Babayemi, S. O. (1980).** *Egungun among the Oyo Yoruba.* Ibadan: Board Publications.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Ancestral masquerade guilds, sacred forest enclosures (`Idi-Eégún`), Alágbàá custody, and funerary transition ethics.
22. **Nwafor, Okechukwu (2021).** *Aso Ebi: Cloth and Sociability in Urban Nigeria.* Ann Arbor: University of Michigan Press.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Contemporary and pre-colonial sociability, collective kinship uniform textiles, and economic reciprocity.
23. **Ajayi, J. F. A., and Robert S. Smith (1964).** *Yoruba Warfare in the Nineteenth Century.* Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Tactical analysis of the Ìjàyè War (1860–1862), siege fortifications, firearm integration, and pre-battle councils (`Ipado-Ogun`).
24. **Awe, Bolanle (1992).** *Nigerian Women in Historical Perspective.* Lagos: Sankore / Bookcraft.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Female political autonomy, maternal diplomacy, market leadership (`Ìyálòde`, `Ọ̀tun-Ìyál'óde`), and royal harem governance (`Bààfin`).
25. **Bascom, William (1951).** *Yoruba Food & Cooking.* Africa 21, no. 1: 41–53.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Indigenous gastronomy, pulse and cereal preparations (`Ògùdù`, `Ọ̀pọ̀-Ẹ̀wà`), and agrarian communal banqueting.
26. **Vlach, John Michael (1984).** *The Brazilian House in Nigeria: The Emergence of a Bilingual Architecture.* Journal of American Folklore 97: 3–23.
    - *Engine Coverage:* 19th-century Saro and Aguda returnee masonry, Afro-Brazilian baroque stucco (`Arowo-Ilé`), and hybrid compound architecture.
27. **Hermon-Hodge, H. B. (1929).** *Gazetteer of Ilorin Province.* London: Allen & Unwin.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Frontier defense chronologies, northern imperial treaties, and the historical records of `Afọ̀njá of Ìlọrin`.
28. **Akinyele, I. B. (1911).** *Iwe Itan Ibadan (The History of Ibadan).* Exeter: James Townsend & Sons.
    - *Engine Coverage:* Primary dynastic registers of Ìbàdàn military chieftains and civic administrators.
